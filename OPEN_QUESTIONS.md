## OQ-008  D-065's auto-accept ceiling is declared "per period" but is compared against all-history cumulative movement
Raised: 2026-08-24   Task: repairing the four test failures caused by the 2026-08-24 decision resolutions (corpus/00 section 2b, corpus/06 section 4.2)
Blocked by: DECISION-REQUIRED[D-065] | conflict between D-065's stated basis and `src/mapping/engine.py`'s implementation
What I need: is D-065's ₹1,00,000 ceiling measured against one month's movement, twelve months' movement, or all movement ingested to date?
The number is declared and unambiguous; the basis it is measured on is not, and the three candidate bases differ by more than an order of magnitude on the reference company. `corpus/06` section 4.2 states the condition as "the account's period value below a declared rupee ceiling (D-065: flat ₹1,00,000 per period)". `src/mapping/engine.py`'s `evaluate_auto_accept` compares against `AccountToMap.period_value_inr`, which `extract_coa`'s own docstring defines as "the sum of absolute `amount_base` movement across every ingested fact for that account", noting that this sprint ingests full history in one pass so corpus/06 section 4.1's "twelve months of movement" "collapses to 'all movement seen so far'". For the synthetic manufacturer that is 36 months, so the gate is currently roughly three times stricter than a twelve-month reading and thirty-six times stricter than a monthly one — and the divergence grows with every month ingested, which means the same ledger auto-accepts today and stops auto-accepting later without anything about it changing.
Measured on the reference company (₹100 Cr revenue in year one, 36 months ingested): 77 accounts match an exact rule; 24 fall below the ceiling and auto-accept, 53 go to human review. The median rule-matched ledger carries ₹1.34 Cr of cumulative movement, 134x the ceiling; "Sales - Direct (North)" carries ₹98.4 Cr, 9,838x. Even read as a single month, the median ledger is roughly ₹37 lakh, still 37x the ceiling. So on any of the three bases a flat ₹1,00,000 auto-accepts only the near-dormant tail of a mid-market company's ledger, and every material account is a human's confirmation. That may well be the intent — it is the conservative reading, and it is consistent with this system's purpose — but it is a large change in what the auto-accept loop is for, it was not stated as the intent in D-065, and it should be confirmed rather than assumed from a number that was chosen to answer a different question.
Options I can see: (a) per calendar month, the strictest and most stable reading, and the only one where a ledger's auto-accept eligibility does not drift as history accumulates; (b) twelve months of movement, matching what corpus/06 sections 4.1 and 5 already say the proposer and the review queue are shown, at the cost of the ceiling meaning something different in month three than in month thirteen; (c) all movement ingested to date, the current behaviour, which makes the gate strictly tighten over a tenant's life; (d) the ceiling is confirmed as intentionally admitting only immaterial ledgers on the current basis, in which case corpus/06 section 4.2's "per period" wording should be corrected to say so.
What I will NOT do: pick a basis and normalise `period_value_inr` to it. Which window a materiality ceiling is measured over is a threshold decision, and CLAUDE.md section 3.2 forbids inventing one; dividing by 36 to "make it per period" would be exactly that.
Downstream blocked: nothing is blocked from running. `tests/integration/test_mapping_auto_accept_judgement_class.py::test_non_judgement_exact_matches_do_auto_accept` was rewritten to assert the acceptance *rule* against whatever basis is in force rather than a fixed count, so it passes under any resolution and will not need changing again unless the rule itself changes. What is affected is the review queue's size on a real onboarding: under the current basis a first mapping run hands a human roughly two thirds of the material ledger, which is a workload question worth knowing before a pilot rather than during one.

## OQ-009  A load touching an already-validated period has no stated transition
Raised: 2026-08-30   Task: Sprint 3, wiring OPEN -> VALIDATED into `load_gl_file` (corpus/09 section 5)
Blocked by: corpus gap
What I need: what should a GL load do when it touches a period that already carries a period_lock row -- usually VALIDATED, sometimes MAPPED?
`src/quality/period_state.validate_period` refuses it today, and refuses correctly on the corpus as written: corpus/09 section 5 draws five arrows and no self-arrow on any state, so VALIDATED -> VALIDATED is not a transition the diagram admits, and `_PREDECESSOR` implements that diagram literally. But a second GL file for the same month is ordinary usage, not an error -- a corrected export, a month re-sent after the client posts late vouchers, or the trailing ninety-day re-pull that corpus/09 section 5 itself calls "the normal case in Indian accounting, not an exception." The synthetic manufacturer already does it: defect #1 puts backdated entries with `voucher_date` in 2024-02 inside the 2026-01 file, so loading month 33 touches a period that was validated twenty-two months earlier. With the transition wired into the load, every one of those re-uploads hits a refusal on a path where nothing is actually wrong.
corpus/09's only sentence about a period moving backwards is about LOCKED: "If a locked period becomes unreconciled because new data arrived, that is a restatement event with a reason, an owner and a visible delta, not a status flag quietly flipping." That names a mechanism, restatement, and gives it a state, RESTATED. There is no equivalent sentence, and no state, for a VALIDATED or MAPPED period receiving new data. The corpus is explicit at one end of the machine and silent at the other, and the silence is exactly where every ordinary re-upload lands.
Options I can see: (a) skip the transition when a lock row already exists, and record on `LoadResult` that the load touched an already-validated period, so the caller sees it happened rather than the load quietly looking identical to a first upload; (b) re-run the blocking checks and append another `validated` row despite the diagram drawing no self-arrow, treating VALIDATED as re-enterable; (c) treat this as needing a mechanism the corpus does not have -- the VALIDATED/MAPPED analogue of restatement, with a reason and an owner -- in which case neither (a) nor (b) is right and the wiring waits for it.
The cost of (a) is specific and worth stating rather than discovering later: the blocking checks do not re-run. The period keeps the `validated` row it already had, and that row was earned by a check that ran against a different set of facts than the ones now in `fact_gl_entry`. A period can therefore keep serving as "structurally sound" on the strength of a check that never saw the data it is now describing. (b) removes that at the price of a transition section 5 does not draw. (c) removes it properly and blocks the work.
What I will NOT do: pick (b) on my own judgement, or let the load swallow the refusal so a re-upload is indistinguishable from a first upload. Whether a period may re-enter a state is a state-machine question and section 5 is the authority on it; inventing a sixth arrow is not a branch to add in `load_pipeline.py`.
Downstream blocked: nothing is blocked from running. `src/quality/period_state.py`'s refusal is unchanged and still correct in isolation -- this is about what the caller does with it. Until this resolves, the wired transition is the only place in the system where an ordinary second upload of a month meets a hard refusal, and the choice made here decides whether a re-validated period is possible at all.

**Resolved 2026-08-30 by Bhavya Singhal, founder.** Option (a), provisional until the corpus says otherwise. `src/ingest/load_pipeline.load_gl_file` now attempts OPEN -> VALIDATED once per period in `periods_touched`, and where a period_lock row already exists it skips the transition and records a `PeriodTransitionOutcome` (`transitioned=False`) on `LoadResult.period_transitions`, carrying the status it found and the reason.
